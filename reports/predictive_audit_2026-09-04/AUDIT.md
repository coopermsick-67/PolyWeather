**Implementation follow-up (September 5 UTC):** The original audit below is preserved as a dated baseline. Subsequent correctness fixes, model comparisons, the corrected threshold experiment, and remaining blockers are recorded in [the current findings](../accuracy_improvement_2026-09-05/FINDINGS.md). Higher hypothetical one-sided threshold hit rates do **not** establish 85% forecast accuracy, executable-market wins, calibrated probabilities, or profits.

**Verdict: useful retrospective temperature correction; not a demonstrated 85% betting system.** The saved evaluation has measurable skill, but source duplication, an unmatched forecast issuance contract, disconnected live serving, and an unvalidated decision layer prevent a credible operational win-rate claim. Recomputed numbers describe the supplied historical experiment, including its defects. They do not validate the model's advertised input identities or live performance.

**The 10 best things, ranked**

1. Official NOAA/NCEI TMAX labels provide a concrete observable target instead of another forecast masquerading as truth. Provider-specific settlement equivalence still needs verification.
2. Explicit station IDs and local timezones avoid city-center substitution; Midway, Hobby, and Central Park are represented explicitly.
3. The numerical-weather baseline plus learned residual is a sensible forecasting architecture. It corrects physical guidance rather than attempting weather prediction from a short temperature series alone.
4. The stored XGBoost experiment reduces MAE by 27.40% versus its raw NBM baseline. This is relative error reduction, not a win rate.
5. It beats that baseline at all 20 stations, rather than relying on one easy location to carry the aggregate.
6. It also beats Ridge and the station blend overall: MAEs 1.7503°F, 1.8803°F, and 1.7574°F respectively. The gain over the blend is small and should not be oversold.
7. Seventeen expanding-origin folds preserve chronological target separation, and held-out station-date rows do not repeat.
8. Station identity, calendar cycles, hourly profile shape, and weather-regime features are present. This is useful structure, though the duplicate guidance fields undermine some disagreement features.
9. The code separates a trailing calibration block from estimator fitting and measures asymmetric interval coverage. Every station exceeds the current 75% severe-undercoverage floor; this is empirical evidence, not a universal coverage guarantee.
10. Row-level artifacts, model manifests, source caches, explicit SHADOW_ONLY status, and passing tests make the system auditable. This audit could reproduce the headline result instead of taking it on faith.

**The 20 worst things, ranked by decision impact**

1. **Critical — the supposed guidance diversity is largely duplicated.** HRRR and GFS Tmax match exactly on 10,259/10,279 held-out rows (99.8054%); their forecast-error correlation is 0.9970. Three sampled raw source files also contain 744/744 identical hourly temperatures, clouds, and winds for their first location. Source aliasing, fallback, or collection root cause is unresolved. These data do not support a claim of three independent guidance sources. Source: `contract_reproductions.json`; `data.py:249`; raw paths recorded in the JSON.
2. **Critical — the public app was not serving the audited candidate.** The September 5 forecast returned all 20 stations as `LIVE_NWS_FORECAST`, zero `isCalibrated=true`, and no bet decisions. The default Render health URL returned 404. This proves the observed serving state, not that no alternate backend exists. Snapshot modelVersion `81ded14f0a180cf9` also differs from the current local artifact hash prefix `b57b01aaeaaafaa0`; fallback metadata is not proof of the model actually running. Source: saved live snapshots; `create-worker.mjs:62`.
3. **Critical — training and live issuance contracts differ.** Previous-day1 is a 24-hour lead relative to each valid hour. A daily maximum aggregates different knowledge cutoffs; it is not one forecast available at a fixed decision time. Live inference instead uses the latest feed and labels tomorrow as one day ahead regardless of request hour. Source: `data.py:471`, model manifest, Open-Meteo Previous Runs documentation.
4. **Critical — no measured operational betting win rate.** The local workspace has no shadow JSONL forecast ledger or resolved bet ledger. Dashboard refresh state is not a fixed-cutoff performance record. Existing backtest/filter utilities do not substitute for scored live decisions, including rejected and unavailable opportunities. Source: `shadow.py`, `betfilter/backtest.py`, local data/artifact inventory.
5. **Critical — the recommendation probability gate is unreachable.** Scanning every possible two-degree window in each current empirical station distribution yields a maximum calibrated probability of 56.9514% (Las Vegas). Standard, conservative, and very-conservative gates demand 58%, 63%, and 68%. The current supported day-ahead/default-bucket path therefore cannot recommend any station in any mode. This does not justify lowering thresholds; it exposes the absence of conditional concentration needed for the product. Source: `probability_ceiling.csv`; `config.py:60`; `decision.py:258`.
6. **High — uncertainty cannot become sharper on an easier day.** The live empirical distribution shifts the same pooled station residual history around each new point forecast. Its scale does not depend on today's cloud regime, forecast spread, season, or issue hour. Scoring agreement separately does not change this distribution. Source: `bet_evidence.py:55`; `dashboard_payload.py:420`.
7. **High — probability calibration breaks the distribution.** Every candidate bucket is independently remapped through a binary calibrator. A reproducible Miami/80.5°F example changes total enumerated mass from 99.8051% to 115.2684%. The results are incoherent as mutually exclusive outcome probabilities, and gap/entropy scoring then consumes them. Source: `decision.py:344`; `contract_reproductions.json`.
8. **High — the shipped probability correction has no demonstrated later holdout.** The JSON records 10,279 fitted rows through September 1, matching the entire scored cohort. I found no later validation artifact or production fitting command tying the correction to the current empirical distribution. The file lacks source-prediction hash and distribution-method identity. This is missing evidence, not proof of future leakage during current inference. Source: production_v4 calibrator JSON; `bet_evidence.py:152`.
9. **High — market-contract verification does not bind the final decision.** The outer forecast sets `rules_available=False` and NO BET, but the inner engine gets `settlement_station_verified=True` and an invented default bucket. It never receives a verified provider contract. Other gates currently prevent recommendations, but the invariant is missing. Source: `dashboard_payload.py:442`, `:485`, `:563`.
10. **High — the quality score is a hand-built heuristic.** The 30/15/15/... weights and 82/90 tier boundaries are not an estimated probability of success. No real held-out filter-effectiveness artifact establishes that an 85/100 score means 85% accuracy. Source: `betfilter/config.py`; `decision.py:194`; synthetic filter tests.
11. **High — the model and bucket selector optimize different targets.** XGBoost minimizes absolute temperature error; the app rounds that point to a bucket instead of necessarily selecting the bucket with the highest conditional probability. A good median need not maximize the probability of a narrow interval. Source: `model.py:374`; `bet_evidence.py:39`; `dashboard_payload.py:442`.
12. **High — no validated same-day forecasting model.** Only lead day 1 is supported. Today uses numerical guidance; observations chiefly impose a floor. Heating-curve utilities exist but the live adapter does not pass a learned heating curve or local hour. There is no validated remaining-heating distribution. Source: `model.py:62`; `dashboard_payload.py:508`; `betfilter/observation.py`.
13. **High — partial hourly profiles masquerade as complete daily forecasts.** Sixty training rows have less than 90% temperature-profile availability; all pass the current feature-completeness and core-Tmax gates. Minimum NBM availability is 16.67%. A finite maximum from four hours is not proof of a valid whole-day maximum. Source: `partial_profiles.csv`; `data.py:223`; `model.py:109`.
14. **High — freshness measures download time, not forecast age.** `source_fetched_at_utc` is set to the current time and drives the stale-data gate. Old guidance downloaded now therefore appears fresh; unknown age also does not fail that gate. Source: `data.py:514`; `dashboard_payload.py:455`; `decision.py:245`.
15. **High — refresh frequency can manufacture stability credit.** Two identical snapshots one second apart produce stability=1.0 and a reported six-hour change of zero. Elapsed coverage and independent source-run changes are not required. Source: `betfilter/stability.py:83`; saved reproduction.
16. **High — the displayed forecast is not the scored forecast.** The UI holds one-degree revisions and translates the interval to the held display high, while historical accuracy scores the raw, unheld prediction. No replay establishes the resulting display policy's hit rate or interval coverage. Source: `dashboard_payload.py:176`, `:606`.
17. **High — regional and tail errors are large enough to dominate narrow markets.** San Francisco hits its default bucket only 26.46%; Washington 29.18%; even Las Vegas tops out at 53.50%. There are 500 errors greater than 5°F, a 15.36°F maximum error, and a +0.62°F mean bias at SFO. The 1.75°F headline hides meaningful failure concentration. Source: city_accuracy.csv; largest_errors.csv.
18. **Medium/high — history and adaptation evidence are thin.** Training spans only 694 dates, under two years. The current estimator ends July 3; the later 60 days update offsets/intervals rather than the learned weather relationships. Model variants have repeatedly used these historical periods, so a fresh untouched test is essential. This does not establish that a particular hyperparameter leaked. Source: manifest; model.py:357; historical artifact families.
19. **High — fallback paths can produce incorrect forecast values or stale evidence.** `Number(null)` becomes zero and passes the worker's finite check. The fallback also spreads prior forecast/snapshot objects without explicitly clearing every bet decision/summary field, and labels best_match recovery as NBM. These are reproducible code defects or latent paths; no fabricated zero or stale positive recommendation was observed in the captured live responses. Source: `create-worker.mjs:124`, `:129`, `:194`, `:210`.
20. **High — winning money is not in the decision inputs.** The engine receives no executable market price, payout schedule, fees, liquidity, ticket composition, or cross-leg dependence. Financial-result helpers exist but do not drive forecast selection or the supplied reliability history. Neither bucket hits nor quality scores establish positive expected returns. Source: BetEvidence in decision.py; results.py; bet_evidence.py.

**Ordered plan toward 85%**

The proposed primary target is an 85% exact-contract hit rate on predeclared, selected, executable recommendations, with all 20 cities tracked. This is different from an 85% within-2°F forecast rate across every city-day, and from an 85% financial ticket win rate. If every city must be picked every day, current evidence does not support a credible promise of 85%.

1. **Freeze the success contract and baseline.** Specify provider, station, official settlement product, date boundary, rounding, bucket grid, entry cutoff, ties/voids, and whether success means leg hits or profitable tickets. Publish separate within-2°F, exact-contract, selection-coverage, and net-return metrics. Preserve this audit as the initial baseline.
2. **Repair serving and unify all decision gates.** Connect the intended artifact to the public API; assert model identity and evidence family on every response. Make unverified contracts, stale source runs, missing probability calibration, and fallbacks incapable of carrying recommendations. Fix null-to-zero, mislabeled-source, and stale copied-evidence paths. Exit: all 20 stations explicitly report valid candidate output or unavailable/fallback states with matching metadata.
3. **Resolve guidance duplication and rebuild the data contract.** Retrieve and compare HRRR/GFS independently with known run identities, trace saved payloads to their true upstream models, then regenerate invalid features. Archive run initialization, release/availability time, retrieval time, valid hours, grid/elevation policy, source product/version, and checksum. Enforce expected local-day hour coverage, including DST; validate label flags and settlement revisions. Exit: source identities and time cutoffs are reproducible for every scored row.
4. **Match historical and live issuance times; start immutable logging.** Use fixed operational cutoffs and only data available by each cutoff. Open-Meteo now documents individual-run access by initialization time; direct NOAA archives are another candidate. Log predictions, probabilities, decisions, rejected opportunities, and outages before settlement. Keep full histories of source-run changes independent of user refreshes. Archive collection can start while subsequent model work proceeds.
5. **Rebuild honest benchmarks and independent evaluation.** Compare raw and simple station-bias-corrected NBM, Ridge, current XGBoost, genuine complementary guidance, and available operational MOS/NWS products on the same cutoff and target. Use separate chronological estimator training, probability calibration, threshold selection, and untouched testing. Report performance by city, season, horizon, weather regime, and outage state; bootstrap multi-date blocks.
6. **Model the whole conditional outcome distribution.** Test quantile residual models or a coherent discrete temperature distribution conditioned on station, issue hour, season, true model disagreement, clouds/radiation, wind/advection, and precipitation timing. Preserve weather-based predictors only when prior-data ablations demonstrate benefit. Replace independent bucket remapping with coherent distribution calibration; verify nonnegative probabilities summing to one including tails, and evaluate Brier/log scores, calibration, sharpness, and exact-contract hits on later data.
7. **Build and validate a separate same-day model.** Estimate remaining daily maximum from observations available at fixed morning/noon/afternoon cutoffs, raw guidance, cloud evolution, and heating trends. Verify station precision and official settlement differences. Later forecasts can be easier only if the actual market is still executable then. This is a hypothesis to test, not an assumed accuracy boost.
8. **Improve the difficult stations and learn when to abstain.** Prioritize SFO, DCA, Denver, Boston, and other weak cohorts. Test coastal/frontal specialists and partial pooling with sufficient samples. Learn selection from genuine out-of-time errors; fix elapsed-time stability and source-age checks. Require coherent calibrated probabilities and positive expected value after actual terms. Set a minimum selection-coverage floor before inspecting the final test; zero picks means no measured win rate.
9. **Freeze the model and policy, then prove the target prospectively.** Proposed starting evidence requirement: at least 500 resolved selected picks across at least 90 distinct dates, with declared coverage and seasonal/regime diversity; extend until dependence-aware precision is adequate. Publish all 20 city results, including zero-pick cities. Require a one-sided 95% date-block lower confidence bound of at least 85% for a substantiated aggregate claim, plus positive net returns. A raw 85% estimate alone cannot pass that lower-bound criterion. For scale, 450/500 hits gives a two-sided Wilson lower bound around 87.1% under independence; real weather dependence can require more evidence. Each city needs its own evidence before claiming 85% in every city.
10. **Promote only the validated scope and monitor it.** Monitor source identity, missingness, market availability, calibration drift, selection coverage, city/regime hit rates, and economic returns. Revert or abstain when gates fail. Keep challenging the model in shadow; require new out-of-time evidence after material changes. If the target fails, report the achieved rate and coverage rather than lowering the standard after seeing results.

If the intended target is instead 85% within 2°F on every city-day, the same data and validation repairs apply, but selection cannot count as improvement. That objective needs an additional 17.11 percentage points over the saved baseline. For the default narrow bucket, the increase is 46.93 points. Neither is a small threshold adjustment. The current sample reaches 85% only by widening the absolute-error tolerance to roughly 3.14°F; that changes the task and is not success at the requested accuracy.
